About me

I’m Hari. Music is what got me into electrical engineering. I wanted to understand the math that describes sound, and that turned into a love of signal processing. That artistic side still shapes how I work, in audio and well beyond it.

Coming at the math from that side has been behind my best work, in my own projects and in my capstone, a real-time spatial audio engine for VR. It has spread well past audio since: most of my other projects ended up on a lab bench, from a motor I wound to a high-current MOSFET supply and an RTOS control loop.

I finish my BESc at Western in December 2026 and can start any time after December 23. I’m looking for entry-level roles in power systems, embedded systems, IT, or software.

Alongside school, I’ve worked as a Student Border Services Officer with the Canada Border Services Agency since 2023, a job built on accuracy, judgement, and clear reporting to senior officers and partner agencies.

Capstone · Team of two · 2025–2026

Hybrid VR spatial audio engine

Sound in VR that behaves properly around doors and walls. I trained and evaluated the model that refines early room reflections, wrote C++ runtime code in Unreal Engine 5 that reacts to scene geometry and listener position, and moved inference onto a worker thread so it couldn’t stall the audio callback.

0.413 msmean standalone inference, TensorRT FP16
1.373 dBC50 error on 1,000 held-out synthetic scenes
9,000paired room responses used for training

Power electronics · Team · 2025

Spot welder power supply

100 Adesign target for the supply

An Arduino-controlled supply that switches high- and low-side MOSFETs through an IR2011 gate driver. I traced the switching and bootstrap faults with a scope and multimeter and found a supply-routing error in the gate-driver circuit. We then adjusted dead time and gate resistance and corrected the PWM timing.

Power systems lab · 2025

Power flow and voltage stability

0.962 p.u.mid-line bus voltage at 700 MW, down from 1.026 at 100 MW

A PowerWorld model of a generator feeding an infinite bus over a 400 kV line, stepped from 100 to 700 MW. By the last step, losses were about 33 MW and the generator had gone from absorbing 50 Mvar to supplying 208 Mvar.

PowerWorld, power flow analysis
